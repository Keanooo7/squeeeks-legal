What we collect

  • Account details — your email address and a user ID, created when you sign up.
  • Sign in with Apple — if you choose it, Apple gives us a user identifier and an email address. If you pick Hide My Email, that address is an Apple relay address and we never see your real one. We do not receive your Apple ID password.
  • Your age, checked once and not kept — when you create an account we ask for your year of birth only to confirm you are 13 or older. We do not store your year of birth, your date of birth or your age.
  • Profile information — the display name and avatar you choose.
  • Game data — your cleaning tasks, streaks, house layout, in-game currency, and friend connections.
  • Family chat and household data — if you create or join a family, the messages you post in its chat (the text, who sent it and when), the chores assigned within it, and who belongs to it. Messages are checked by an automatic word filter before they are posted.
  • Reports and blocks — if you report a message or a player, we keep the report (who reported what, the reason you chose, and when) so we can review it. If you block someone, we keep the block so it can be enforced.
  • Device token — if you allow notifications, a push token so reminders can reach your device.
  • Purchase history — records of in-app purchases and subscriptions. Payment card details are handled entirely by Apple and are never seen by us.
  • Crash reports — if the app crashes, a diagnostic report is sent to Google's Firebase Crashlytics so the fault can be found and fixed. The report contains the error, the point in the code it happened, the device model and the OS version, and a short trail of what you had just done in the app — for example builder.opened — recorded as plain event names with no details attached. Crash reports are not linked to your account: they identify an installation of the app, not you. They are not used for advertising, are never combined with data from anywhere else, and are not shared with anyone but Google, acting for us.

We do not collect precise location, contacts, health, biometric or demographic data. If you take an optional photo of a room you have cleaned, it stays on your device and is never uploaded. Your password is handled by Firebase Authentication when you sign in; we do not store it and cannot see it.

Fonts. The app downloads its two typefaces (Fredoka and Nunito) from Google Fonts the first time they are needed. That request goes from your device to Google's servers, so Google receives your device's IP address and the usual technical details of a web request. No account information is sent with it.

Who can see your information

Your profile and house layout are visible only to friends you have accepted. Settings contains a Be discoverable toggle; turning it off makes your account unsearchable by display name.

If you join a family, its members can see your display name and avatar, the messages you post in the family chat, and the chores assigned within it. People outside the family cannot.

You can report a message or a player, and block a player, from inside the app. A blocked player can no longer send you a friend request, a gift or a family message, and no longer appears in your searches. Reports are read by us and are not shown to the person you reported.

Data is stored using Google Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Functions, Cloud Messaging, and Crashlytics), which processes it on our behalf. Apple processes in-app purchases and, if you use it, Sign in with Apple. Google Fonts serves the app's typefaces, as described above.

Where your data is processed

Google processes Firebase data on servers in the United States. For users in the UK and the European Economic Area, that is a transfer outside your home country, and it needs a legal mechanism.

Google states that it complies with the EU–U.S. and Swiss–U.S. Data Privacy Frameworks and the UK Extension to the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and additionally offers Standard Contractual Clauses through its Cloud Data Processing Addendum. Those are the mechanisms this transfer relies on.

In the language of the GDPR and of California law, we are the controller (or business) and Google is our processor (or service provider). Google publishes this position itself at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

How long we keep it

We do not set a fixed number of days. Instead:

  • Your account, profile and game data are kept for as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, they are deleted with it.
  • Family chat messages stay in the family's chat while the family exists. When you delete your account, the messages you sent are deleted too.
  • Reports are kept for as long as we need them to deal with the report and with any repeat behaviour by the same account.
  • Purchase records are kept for as long as needed to honour an active entitlement or subscription, and to deal with refund and billing questions afterwards.
  • Crash reports are held by Google Crashlytics under Google's own retention schedule, not ours. They are not linked to your account and are deleted on Google's cycle.
  • Backups. After deletion, residual copies may persist for a limited period in Google's backup systems before they age out. That schedule is Google's and we do not control it — we cannot promise a number we are not in a position to enforce.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to do the following, and we will not treat you any differently for asking:

  • Access — get a copy of the personal data we hold about you.
  • Rectification — have anything inaccurate corrected. You can change your avatar yourself in Settings; email us for anything else.
  • Erasure — delete your account and its data. You can do this yourself at any time from Settings → Account & Data → Delete account. Deletion removes your account and associated game data and cannot be undone.
  • Portability — receive the data you gave us in a machine-readable form, or ask us to send it elsewhere. The app has no export button today, so email us and we will put the file together by hand.
  • Restriction — ask us to pause what we do with your data while a question about it is being sorted out.
  • Objection — object to our processing where we rely on legitimate interests, which for us means crash reporting.
  • Withdraw consent — turn off notifications at any time in iOS Settings. Withdrawing does not affect anything done before you withdrew.

Email bkbrohon795@gmail.com to exercise any of these. We may need to confirm you control the account's email address before we act, so that nobody else can make a request about your data.

If you are in the UK or the EEA and you think we have got this wrong, you have the right to complain to your data protection supervisory authority. In the UK that is the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk); in the EEA it is the authority in the country where you live. You can complain without contacting us first, though we would rather have the chance to fix it.

How we protect your data

We would rather describe what actually exists than make claims we cannot stand behind:

  • Access to your data is controlled by server-side security rules, so what your device is allowed to read and write is enforced on the server rather than trusted from the app.
  • Accounts are authenticated through Firebase Authentication. We never see or store your password.
  • All traffic between the app and our services runs over encrypted connections (TLS).
  • No card data ever reaches us. Apple handles payment entirely.
  • Storage and infrastructure security is Google's, under the Firebase terms.

No service can promise perfect security, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

If something goes wrong

If a breach of personal data happens and it is likely to be a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will report it to the relevant supervisory authority without undue delay, and within 72 hours of becoming aware of it where the GDPR requires that. If the risk to you is high, we will tell you directly as well, without undue delay. Where other laws set their own notification duties, we will follow those too.
